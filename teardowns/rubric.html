Teardowns

How to read a teardown (and how I'd grade one)

A teardown isn't a product summary — a chatbot does those. It's a point of view you can argue with, evidence from using the thing, an estimate with its assumptions on show, and a bet worth defending. Here's the bar I hold my own to, and the one I'd hold a PM's work to.

Six dimensions, three levels

DimensionAPMPMSPM
1. Thesis Describes the product. No point of view, or a preference stated as fact. Has a view, but it's a complaint or a generic truth. One arguable claim that could be wrong — and the rest of the teardown tests it.
2. User & moment Talks about "users" in general; lists features. Names a user and a job, but stays broad. One user, one job in their words, one decisive moment, and the constraint that makes it hard.
3. Business grounding No mention of how the product makes money. Names the model; picks a metric but no guardrail; no estimate, or a made-up number. Model → the one metric that matters → a guardrail that could disprove the bet → an estimate with assumptions shown.
4. Evidence & prioritisation A screen-by-screen tour; everything matters equally. Real observations, but secondhand or unranked. Two or three first-hand, specific, ideally quantified moments, ranked by impact with a reason.
5. The bet A wish list of features. One solution, but ignores trade-offs or why it isn't already built. A sized bet, its trade-off, the metric it should move, and an honest answer to "why haven't they done it?"
6. Intellectual honesty No prior art; the idea is presented as obviously right. Mentions some prior art; weak on own blind spots. Credits what already exists (and which half it solves), and names the data or failure mode that would kill the bet.

Eight questions I ask when someone walks me through one

  1. What's the one sentence someone could disagree with? No sentence, no teardown.
  2. Whose job is this, in their words, and what makes the moment hard?
  3. How does this product actually make money, and does your bet help or hurt that?
  4. Which single metric moves if you're right? What guardrail says you're wrong?
  5. Is your evidence first-hand, or repeated from reviews and press?
  6. If you could fix only one thing, which, and why that one?
  7. Why hasn't the team already done your bet? Give the most charitable reason.
  8. What would have to be true for you to be wrong, and how would you check?

The red pen

Each teardown has a Show red pen toggle. Turn it on and margin notes appear, each tagged with the dimension above, explaining why that part is built the way it is. Every teardown also ends with a What would drop this to PM level box — the weaker version of each part, and why it falls short. The clean read is the default; the red pen is for anyone learning the craft.
